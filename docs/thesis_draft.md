## Abstract (draft)

Producing a Building Information Model (BIM) for an individual house in India needs a BIM engineer who knows
what to ask the client, which code clauses govern, how to lay out the rooms and how to design the structure.
Most people building a house have none of this. Recent work shows that a large language model (LLM) can turn a
short text prompt into a building model by writing code against a BIM API [CITE Text2BIM]. Such models,
however, ignore building regulations and structural design, and cannot guarantee a usable plan.

STRATA addresses both gaps with a pipeline in which the LLM only proposes, and engineering tools check, decide
and execute:

1. **Brief.** A chatbot guided by a 76-item pre-BIM information-requirements matrix collects the brief.
2. **Spec.** A knowledge graph of NBC 2016, IS code and Guwahati bylaw clauses fills every value the client
   cannot give, each with its clause, page and quoted text.
3. **Layout.** A genetic algorithm (GA) lays out the plan under hard constraints. An independent rule checker
   verifies it, and the user approves it.
4. **Structure.** STAAD.Pro analyses and designs the RCC frame to IS 456, IS 875, IS 1893 and IS 13920 in an
   automatic resize loop.
5. **Services and finishes.** Plumbing, electrical and finishing layers are added from room templates and
   code rules.
6. **Revit.** A deterministic pyRevit script builds a native Revit 2027 model, which is exported to IFC and
   checked back against the STAAD design.

The pipeline was evaluated on 25 synthetic client briefs:
- **Chatbot:** extraction precision 0.99, and 20 of 25 briefs complete and correct.
- **Delivered designs:** none of the 25 runs delivered a design that breaks a code rule or fails the
  structural design. Four were delivered in about one minute each, each passing every rule check and STAAD
  with a valid building model.
- **Stops:** 20 runs stopped with brief changes that would fit, and one was refused because its city has no
  bylaw layer.
- **Weakness:** coverage on narrow (25–30 ft) plots, where the GA's plan grammar fails but a designer's plan
  passes.
- **Baseline comparison:** pending; see §8.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

- **BIM in India.** BIM represents a building digitally across its life cycle [CITE Eastman 2011; Azhar 2011].
  It is mandated for large public projects in India [CHECK MoHUA reference], but is rare in private
  residential work. Skilled BIM engineers are scarce and expensive, and the drawing-based workflow is
  entrenched.
- **The individual house.** Most construction in India is individual houses on small plots, G+1 or G+2, with
  an RCC frame and brick infill. The owner usually talks to a contractor or a draftsman. Code compliance, if
  checked at all, is checked at the building-permit stage.
- **LLMs.** LLMs can now read plain language and produce structured output, and have been used to generate
  building models from text. That makes it plausible that a non-expert could describe a house and get a
  professional model back.

### 1.2 Problem statement

A homeowner who wants a BIM model of their house faces two gaps.

1. **The information gap.** The homeowner does not know what a BIM engineer needs. Plot dimensions and
   orientation, abutting road widths, number of storeys now and later, family composition, rooms, parking,
   soil, services and finishes all shape the design. Many further values come from codes the homeowner has
   never read: setbacks, room minimums, stair geometry, opening ratios, loads, seismic zone, concrete grade
   and covers.
2. **The reliability gap.** An LLM can propose a plan, but it cannot guarantee one:
   - Its rooms may be under the code minimum, and its doors may lead nowhere.
   - Its columns may not stack, and its spans may be unbuildable.
   - It does no structural design at all.

   Code generated by an LLM can also simply fail to run.

### 1.3 Origin and evolution of the project

The project began (August 2026) as the *Intent-to-BIM Intelligence Engine* (IBIE) in a BTP proposal.
- **Original aim:** convert unstructured public-works documents (tender notices, scope-of-work statements,
  detailed project reports) into a "10D" BIM model, covering geometry, schedule, cost, sustainability,
  facility management, safety, lean productivity and risk (`BTP_Proposal_2027.pdf`).
- **Research-intern stage.** The internship report renamed the system STRATA. It compared three existing
  approaches: Text2BIM-style agents, an LLM driving Revit through the Model Context Protocol (MCP), and visual
  3D generators. It argued for a standards-aware inference engine (`Research_Intern_Report_-_BTP_CE4198P.pdf`).
- **Early code (August–September 2026).** A document-ingestion pipeline (PyMuPDF, with PaddleOCR and
  LayoutLMv3 paths), an LLM extractor, and the `strata_demo` prototype. The prototype is a Text2BIM-style
  multi-agent system in which LLM agents write an IfcOpenShell script that produces an IFC file.
- **Supervisor feedback (around 26 October 2026)** redirected the project (§3):
  - target individuals building a house, not tender documents, since large organisations have BIM teams
  - prefer LLMs to training models from scratch
  - prepare a matrix of everything an engineer needs before BIM
  - model five layers (architectural, civil, plumbing, electrical, finishing) to the NBC and bylaws
  - deliver a fully functional native Revit model "by any means"

  The `strata_demo` output, which opens in Revit as non-editable linked geometry, was not acceptable.
- **The present system** (27 October to 20 November 2026) was planned and built after that feedback;
  `project_plan.md` records it phase by phase.

### 1.4 Aim and objectives

**Aim.** Given a plain-language brief from a homeowner, produce a native, editable Revit model of a G+1 or G+2
house. The model must comply with NBC 2016 and the local bylaws and be structurally designed to the IS codes,
and every engineering value in it must be traceable to its source.

**Objectives**
1. Define the information an engineer needs before BIM, by source and layer: the information-requirements
   matrix.
2. Build a brief chatbot that asks a non-expert only for what they can answer and fills the rest with recorded
   defaults.
3. Encode the governing code clauses as a queryable knowledge graph in which every value cites its clause.
4. Generate floor plans that satisfy the codes, with checks independent of the generator and user approval.
5. Design the structure to IS 456, IS 875, IS 1893 and IS 13920 automatically, feeding infeasibility back to
   the layout.
6. Add plumbing, electrical and finishing layers at LOD 200.
7. Build a native Revit model deterministically, export IFC, and check it against the structural design.
8. Evaluate against an LLM-only baseline and against designers' plans.

### 1.5 Scope

The scope was fixed on 27 October 2026.

| Item | In scope | Out of scope (and why) |
|---|---|---|
| Typology | individual residential houses, G+1 and G+2; G+0 with a future floor also works | apartments, institutional (other code parts and occupancies) |
| Location | Guwahati, as a swappable bylaw layer | other cities: no layer, so STRATA refuses rather than guess (§8) |
| BIM layers | architectural, civil/structural, plumbing, electrical, finishing | 4D–10D (future work) |
| Level of development | LOD 200 minimum | LOD 300+ detailing |
| Structure | RCC frame, IS 456 / 875 / 1893 / 13920 via STAAD.Pro | RCDC detailing (GUI-only, §5.5), steel, load-bearing masonry |
| Input | plain-language client brief; optional plot document upload | tender/SoW/DPR documents (code kept, parked) |
| Vastu | recorded in the brief | as a layout objective: future work, decided 11 November 2026 (§5.4) |

### 1.6 Contributions

1. **Information-requirements matrix:** a pre-BIM matrix of 76 items, by source (client, codes, municipality,
   site, engineering defaults) and by BIM layer, with a default and its basis for every item.
2. **Cited knowledge graph:** an RDF graph of NBC 2016, IS code and Assam bylaw clauses.
   - Every value carries its clause, printed page, verbatim quote and a status (transcribed, verified or
     assumed), enforced by SHACL.
   - A mechanical checker matches each quote against the code text.
3. **Architecture:** a pipeline in which the LLM only ever returns data and never code. Layout, structural
   design, the building model and the Revit build are deterministic.
4. **Layout and structure loop:** a GA layout generator whose plans guarantee a stacked column grid and stair
   alignment. Plans are verified by a separate rule checker and fed back from STAAD when a frame cannot be
   designed.
5. **Automated design loop:** STAAD.Pro analysis and IS-code design run in a headless resize loop, with
   strong-column/weak-beam resizing per joint.
6. **Native Revit build:** a deterministic build in Revit 2027 covering all five layers, with design results
   written into element parameters. The IFC export is checked back against STAAD member by member.
7. **Ablation protocol:** an evaluation that separates the contribution of the knowledge graph from that of
   search and verification.

## 2. Related work

*[To be written from the internship report's background section and these pointers.]*

- **LLM to BIM by code generation.** Text2BIM [CITE Du et al. 2025] uses LLM agents (product owner, architect,
  programmer, reviewer) that call high-level BIM APIs in Vectorworks.
  - **Reported results:** 25 design tasks with three LLMs, with rule pass rates above 95% in its own checker
    [CHECK numbers against the paper].
  - **Stated limits:** it excludes building regulations and later-stage engineering detail. STRATA's
    `strata_demo` branch reproduces this approach with IfcOpenShell and is arm A of the ablation.
- **LLM as a Revit co-pilot through MCP.** An MCP server lets an LLM call Revit functions live. This needs a
  human to prompt every rule.
  - **Observed failure:** in the internship test, a flat's main door led nowhere because a room blocked it.
    That observation motivated the door-reachability check (§5.4).
- **Generative 3D models** (e.g. TRELLIS) [CITE]: meshes without parametric or IFC semantics, so not usable
  in AEC.
- **Floor-plan generation.**
  - **Procedural and grammar-based methods** [CITE].
  - **Optimisation:** GA, simulated annealing and MILP [CITE].
  - **Learned generators:** House-GAN, Graph2Plan, diffusion models [CITE], trained on datasets such as RPLAN
    and ResPlan [CITE ResPlan].
  - **The gap:** learned generators need training data matched to the plot form and codes. None targets
    Indian setback-bound plots with NBC minimums.
- **Automated code checking** [CITE Eastman 2009; Solibri; SMC] and **semantic representation of codes:**
  RDF/OWL, LegalRuleML, SHACL [CITE].
- **Automated structural design:** parametric frame generation with FE analysis loops [CITE]. Commercial design
  engines (STAAD.Pro, ETABS, RCDC) implement the IS codes.
- **Synthesis.** No prior system combines brief elicitation for non-experts, cited code inference, verified
  layout, automated IS-code structural design and native Revit output.

## 3. How the scope was decided

This chapter is a narrative of the decisions; Appendix A tabulates them.

### 3.1 From tender documents to a homeowner's brief

- **Original input:** tender, SoW and DPR documents for public works.
- **Problem with that input:** the supervisor observed that organisations producing such documents already
  have BIM teams. The people who most lack BIM expertise are individuals building their own house.
- **Decision (26–27 October):** the input became a conversation with the homeowner.
- **Consequences:**
  - The NLP problem changed from long-document extraction to short-dialogue elicitation.
  - The ingestion code (PyMuPDF, OCR, LayoutLMv3) was parked; PyMuPDF is kept as an optional upload path.
  - A new artefact, the information-requirements matrix, became necessary, to know what to ask.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  - Keep tenders as the main input, rejected for the reason above.
  - Support both inputs, deferred to future work.

### 3.2 From 10D to five layers at LOD 200

- **The 10D ambition:** schedule, cost, energy, facility management, safety, lean and risk, each a research
  topic in itself.
- **Decision:** five BIM layers (architectural, civil/structural, plumbing, electrical, finishing) at LOD 200,
  done properly. 4D–10D become future work.
- **Alternative:** a thin version of every dimension. Rejected because no layer would be defensible.

### 3.3 Native Revit, not IFC-only

- **The prototype's limit:** `strata_demo` writes IFC with IfcOpenShell. In Revit 2027 a linked IFC is
  non-editable geometry, and the supervisor asked for a fully functional native Revit model.
- **Decision:** build native Revit elements with the Revit API from a deterministic script. IFC becomes an
  export from Revit, used for checking.
- **Alternatives:**
  - IFC import with conversion: elements stay generic.
  - LLM-driven Revit through MCP: not deterministic, needs a human in the loop.
  - The IfcOpenShell builder: kept as a future Revit-free path.

### 3.4 The LLM proposes; engineering tools decide

- **The baseline's failures:** the Text2BIM-style baseline gives the LLM full control: it writes the model as
  code. Observed failures included scripts that do not run, rooms below minimum size, and doors leading
  nowhere.
- **Decision:** the LLM only returns schema-validated JSON (data, never code). It is used only where
  understanding language is the job: the brief extraction, the change-request parser, and optional layout
  seeds. Layout scoring, the structural design, the building JSON and the Revit build contain no LLM call.
- **Why:**
  - **Reproducibility:** temperature 0 is not enough on its own, but deterministic code is reproducible.
  - **Verifiability:** every engineering value has a source.
  - **Safety:** no generated code is executed.
- **Alternative:** an LLM with self-review loops, as in Text2BIM's reviewer agent. Rejected because a review
  by the same kind of model doesn't make code compliance certain.

### 3.5 Typology and location

- **Decision:** G+1 and G+2 individual houses in Guwahati.
- **Why:** the commonest private building form; the user's region; a single bylaw text in force (the Assam
  Unified Building Construction (Regulation) Byelaws 2022, amended 2024–2026).
- **Generality:** bylaws are a swappable jurisdiction layer, and national values apply where no local layer
  overrides them.

### 3.6 Which national code

- **The choice:** a copy of the new National Building Construction Standards 2026 (SP 7:2026) was available.
- **Decision:** on 2 November 2026 the supervisor ruled that STRATA cites NBC 2016 (SP 7:2016).
- **Why:** NBCS 2026 leaves room sizes, stairs, FAR, setbacks and parking to local bylaws, so NBC 2016 Part 3
  remains the national source for them.

### 3.7 Vastu

- **Initially:** an optional client constraint.
- **Decision (11 November):** moved to future work. The brief still records the preference.
- **Why:** the plan library (ResPlan) has no orientation, so Vastu rules would have to be hand-written and
  could not be validated against data in the time available.

### 3.8 Pace and scope under the deadline

On 11 November the remaining work exceeded the time left before the 25 November submission. The author chose
to keep the full plan and work faster rather than cut scope, so Phases 0–9 were all attempted.
- **Not done:** the MCP server, the HPC stretch goals and a hand-calculation check of member design (§10).

### 4.3 Data contracts

- **Brief** (`src/brief/schema.py`, pydantic): the matrix's Must and Should items. `sources` records for each
  field whether the client gave it or a default filled it, with the matrix item, so a default never passes
  for an answer.
- **Spec:** requirements by element (room, stair, door, building, plot, structure, services). Each carries a
  comparator, value, unit and source (standard, clause, page, jurisdiction, status). The spec also lists
  `defaults`, `deferred` (values that need the layout first, such as parking from carpet area) and
  `unresolved`.
- **Layout** (`src/spatial/layout.py`, v0.1): floors of room polygons tiling the floor plate (internal edges on
  wall centrelines), doors on shared edges, windows on external edges, columns, stairs. Plot frame: origin at
  the front-left corner seen from the road.
- **Building JSON** (`src/execution/building_schema.py`, v0.5): levels, grids, wall types with layers, walls,
  hosted openings, floors, roofs, stairs, rooms, columns, beams, footings, fixtures, stacks, tank, circuits,
  finishes, and design results.

## 5. Methodology, stage by stage

### 5.1 The information-requirements matrix (Phase 1)

- **What:** 76 items arranged by source (client, codes, municipality/bylaws, site investigation, engineering
  defaults) and by BIM layer. Each item says whether it is Must or Should, how it is obtained, and what is
  assumed if missing ("If missing" column). Shared with the supervisor [link in project notes].
- **Use:** the matrix drives the chatbot (what to ask, what to default) and the knowledge graph (items D1–D18
  are code-derived values).
- **Decision:** only the client can answer some items. These are the Must items A1–A4 and B1, B3–B5: use,
  storeys, family, rooms, plot, orientation, roads and city. Everything else has a documented default.
- **Why:** a non-expert can't answer soil bearing capacity or concrete grade, and shouldn't be asked to.
- **Alternative:** a long questionnaire. Rejected because users abandon it.

### 5.2 Brief elicitation (Phase 2)

**Design**
- **Opening and follow-ups:** the client writes freely. The LLM extracts facts into a schema, and code decides
  what to ask next: only missing Must items, in plain language, one at a time.
- **Units and merging:** code converts units (feet to metres, katha), merges answers, offers defaults for the
  rest, and shows a summary to confirm.
- **Optional question:** preferences (finishes, AC, TV, geyser points) are asked once.

**Decisions**
- *The LLM only extracts; questions, units, merging and the summary are code.* This makes the dialogue
  predictable and testable. The alternative was a free LLM conversation, which can forget items or invent
  values.
- *Plain-words fallbacks in code* for what the model sometimes misses: plot sides ("60 x 80 square feet"),
  road width, "ground floor only".
  - **The incident behind this:** a road misread as 200 ft set a 7.5 m front setback. Roads over 30 m are now
    refused with a message.
- *Schema-valid output with retries:* temperature 0, pinned model, 3 retries with the error, every prompt and
  response logged. Numeric bounds are checked in pydantic, not in the JSON schema sent to the model, because
  Gemma's constrained decoding stalled on bounds.
- *Provider:* Gemma 4 26B on the free tier, chosen for cost. Groq was added as a second provider; the client
  is selected by an environment variable.
- *Plan-form wishes* (courtyard, roof stair, symmetry) are captured in the brief and drive the layout.

**Test set:** 25 synthetic briefs (`evaluation/brief_test_set.json`). They include deliberate omissions,
Hinglish, typos, number words, self-correction, area in katha, an unsure client, another city, a home with a
shop, rental floors and a wheelchair user. Results are in §8.1.

### 5.3 Standards knowledge graph and inference (Phases 1 and 3)

**Content**
- **Codes:** NBC 2016 Parts 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9; IS 456, IS 875 (Parts 1–3, 5), IS 1893, IS 13920; and the Assam
  Unified Building Construction (Regulation) Byelaws 2022 as the Guwahati layer.
- **Bylaw contents:** setbacks, minimum plot, FAR, parking and rainwater harvesting. The 2022 bylaws replaced
  the GMDA 2014 bylaws.
- **Size:** 159 requirements (`docs/kg_signoff.md`).
- **Gating clauses for all five layers:**
  - **Architectural:** room sizes and heights, stairs, ventilation and openings, courtyard, exits and
    corridors, travel distance 30 m, parapet, plinth, ramp.
  - **Civil:** loads, seismic, wind, concrete grade, covers, column steel 0.8–6%, bar sizes, span/depth,
    IS 13920 minimum sizes, strong column factor 1.4.
  - **Plumbing:** water demand, persons per dwelling, overhead tank, septic tank.
  - **Electrical:** sub-circuit limits, lighting and power densities, socket heights, lightning protection.

**Representation**
- **Clause node:** each value is a node with its element, quantity, comparator, value, unit, standard, clause,
  printed page, verbatim quote, jurisdiction and status (transcribed, verified or assumed).
- **Validation:** SHACL shapes (`data/knowledge_graph/shapes.ttl`) enforce that no value lacks a citation.
- **Queries:** 8 SPARQL queries (`src/inference/queries/`): requirements, citations, conditions,
  jurisdictions, local overrides, matrix coverage, by layer, unverified values.

**Inference** (`src/inference/spec.py`)
- **Fill from the brief:** the brief's values are combined with the graph to fill every requirement. The most
  specific jurisdiction governs, and cities without a layer fall back to NBC 2016.
- **Context:** conditions are derived first: occupancy from use, building height from storeys plus plinth,
  road width from the widest road.
- **Deferred values** wait for the layout (parking needs carpet area). Values that cannot be resolved are
  listed, not guessed.

**Decisions**
- *RDF with citations, rather than tables in code.* Every number in the final model can be traced to a page
  of a code, a bylaw can be swapped by loading another layer, and SHACL catches a value without a source.
  - **Alternatives:** constants in code (untraceable), a relational database (no standard for jurisdiction
    layering), or RAG over the PDFs (retrieval and transcription errors at run time, no audit trail).
- *Status per value.* "Assumed" marks interpretations to confirm. Examples:
  - the small-plot setback rule of bye-law 26(3)(iv)
  - FAR, after a 2025 amendment retitled the table
  - moderate exposure for concrete grade
  - Guwahati's climate zone read from a map
- *A mechanical quote check* (`scripts/kg_signoff.py`):
  - **Method:** n-gram matching of each quote against the OCR or PDF text, locating its page.
  - **Coverage:** 142 of 159 quotes found. The rest are tables the OCR garbles, or IS 13920, whose PDF text is
    font-encoded; these were checked by eye on the rendered page.
  - **Errors found:** a wrong page (Annex K seismic zone, p. 121 → 120), and a missing STAAD cover setting
    (beams designed with 25 mm instead of the IS 456 Table 16 value of 30 mm).
  - **Sign-off:** a person signs each value off in `docs/kg_signoff.csv` [CHECK: signed off before
    submission?].
- *Values not encoded:* masonry (IS 1905 not available) stays an engineering default. Eco Niwas Samhita is
  left out: its text was not available, and it is not enforced for houses.

### 5.4 Layout generation (Phase 4)

**Plan library.**
- **Supervisor's suggestion:** floor-plan images from the web.
- **Decision:** use ResPlan, 17,000 South Asian residential plans in vector form under CC BY 4.0 [CITE].
  8,500 were converted to the layout JSON after scale and geometry checks, and 2,148 pass every validity rule.
- **Why:** images would need vectorising (a research problem in itself) and carry copyright problems. ResPlan
  is already vector and openly licensed.
- **Limitation:** ResPlan has no plot sizes or orientation.
- **Reference plans:** four hand-drawn plans for typical Guwahati plots (30×40, 30×50 G+1 and G+2, 40×60),
  drawn for STRATA (`src/spatial/reference_plans.py`), serve as the designer reference in §8.3.

**Retrieval and LLM proposals.**
- `retrieve()` ranks library plans that fit the envelope.
- An LLM call per design focus produces a sketch in the GA's own terms (fixed keys per room). Open-ended lists
  made Gemma loop.
- **Evaluation** (`scripts/eval_seeding.py`):
  - **Raw quality:** 7 of 8 LLM sketches broke NBC rules on their own.
  - **As seeds:** they gave the GA a better first generation but not a better final layout.
- **Decision:** LLM seeding is off by default (`--llm-seeds` turns it on). The orchestrator runs the GA from
  random starts.
- **Library pairing:** only 10 of about 49,000 stair-plan pairs from the library pass the floor-compatibility
  test (stair position, footprint, wall lines). Upper floors therefore come from the GA, not the library.

**Genetic algorithm** (`src/spatial/ga.py`, DEAP)
- **Grammar, "bands and spine":** each floor is cut front to back into 3–5 bands.
  - **Spine:** a 1.2 m passage runs from the front band to the rear, with one room cell each side.
  - **Living room:** spans the front band and holds the main door.
  - **Grid:** a regular structural grid; columns at every grid crossing, identical on all floors; the stair
    cell is shared by all floors.
  - **Narrow plots:** below 6.4 m of width a "side spine" runs along one side, with one room per band.
- **Genome:** random keys in [0, 1] for footprint width, band count, band depths, spine position, stair cell
  and fill direction, plus a floor key and an order key per room. Decoding is deterministic. Attached baths
  are carved from their bedroom's cell, and two small rooms can share a cell.
- **Hard constraints**, by construction or repair where possible:
  - **By construction:** setbacks, reachability, the column grid and stair alignment.
  - **By repair:** band depths are repaired to fit the envelope.
  - **By heavy penalty:** room sizes, stair fit, windows, overflow and FAR.
- **Soft objectives:** comfortable room sizes and proportions, kitchen next to dining, baths over wet rooms, a
  common bath on every bedroom floor, a bedroom downstairs, and little wasted space.
- **Options:** three distinct options. Duplicates are removed by arrangement (which room kind is in which
  cell), and extra restarts run while fewer than three meet every hard constraint.
- **When nothing fits:** `suggest()` tries brief changes (one more storey, kitchen with dining, an attached
  bath made common) and reports which fit.
- **Decisions:**
  - *A grammar rather than free geometry.* Structure (stacked columns, aligned stairs, spans ≤ 5 m) is
    guaranteed, and the search space is small enough for a GA on a laptop. The cost is coverage: forms
    outside the grammar can't be produced (§8.2–8.3).
  - *A GA rather than a learned model:* no training data for Indian setback-bound plots, constraints that
    change by jurisdiction, and a fitness function that explains its reasons ("left row spans 6.1 m").
  - *A GA rather than MILP:* mixed geometric and combinatorial constraints with soft objectives. [Optional:
    MILP as future comparison.]

**Courtyard plan type** (`src/spatial/court.py`).
- **Form:** rooms around a central court open to the sky, ringed by a 1.5 m open verandah, with symmetric
  side bays.
- **When used:** when the client asks for a courtyard, or the plot is too wide for rooms either side of one
  passage.
- **Code check:** court size per NBC Part 3 cl. 8.2.5(a), with this clause added to the graph.
- **Result:** a 60×80 ft single-storey brief gives three passing options, passes STAAD in 5 rounds, and builds
  in Revit with IFC 21/21.

**Windows clear of columns.**
- **Rule:** windows go only on wall stretches 0.35 m clear of every column centre (half a 450 mm column plus a
  jamb), 0.2 m from wall ends and 0.7 m from the main door. The GA's window capacity uses the same stretches,
  and the checker fails any window crossing a column.
- **Origin:** this was found by the author in Revit, where windows coincided with columns.

**Independent rule checker** (`src/spatial/checker.py`)
- **Independence:** a separate code path from the GA that reads only the layout and the spec. The GA cannot
  pass its own homework.
- **Checks:**
  - **Rooms and site:** room area, width and height; the programme; setbacks; overlaps.
  - **Walls and openings:** walls; doors (shared wall, widths, main exit door, WC not opening into the
    kitchen); windows (external wall, opening ratio, bath ventilator).
  - **Circulation and structure:** corridor width; stairs; reachability from the entrance; travel distance
    to an exit (NBC Part 4 Table 5); column and stair continuity; spans.
  - **Totals:** carpet and built-up area, FAR, materials.
- **Citations:** each result cites its clause, or is marked "validity" (a plan that isn't buildable
  otherwise) or "practice".
- **Origin of door reachability:** the MCP experiment in the internship report, where a flat's main door led
  nowhere.

**User approval and change requests** (`src/spatial/revise.py`)
- **Preview:** an SVG preview with checks and areas. The user approves one option, asks for another, or tries
  a suggested brief change.
- **Change requests:**
  - **Parsing:** a free-text request ("move the door of the bathroom next to bedroom 4 into bedroom 4") is
    turned by one LLM call into at most four edits over the plan's own room names. The edit kinds are move to
    a floor, bigger or smaller, next to or away from, swap, terrace end, move a door, or "unsupported". The
    prompt lists which rooms share a wall.
  - **Refusals:** code refuses edits that break a rule and cites the clause. Examples: a door into the living
    room from a service room, a bedroom left without a bath, a shared wall too short for a door.
  - **Re-run:** the GA re-runs seeded from the approved option, and every new option is checked again.
- **Decision:** the LLM translates intent; code enforces rules. The alternative, letting the LLM edit the
  geometry, would re-open the reliability gap.

### 5.5 Structural design with STAAD.Pro (Phase 5)

**Layout to frame** (`src/structure/from_layout.py`)
- **Grid and members:** grid lines on wall centrelines; plinth beams; columns only under each storey's own
  cells (terraces become roofs with parapets); a stair cabin (mumty) storey over the stair well; the overhead
  tank's weight on the cabin roof.
- **Loads:** walls as beam loads net of openings, partitions off the grid as slab loads (IS 875 Part 2
  cl. 3.1.2), and stair loads.

**Initial sizing** (`src/structure/sizing.py`)
- **Members:** beams at span/12, slabs to IS 456 cl. 24.1, columns from tributary load (cl. 39.3), with the
  IS 13920 minimums (beam 200 mm, column 300 mm).
- **Footings:** from STAAD service reactions on the brief's soil bearing capacity (+50% for earthquake
  combinations, IS 1893 cl. 6.3.5.2), checked for punching and one-way shear. Overlapping pads are combined.

**STAAD input** (`src/structure/std_writer.py`)
- **Loads:**
  - **Seismic:** IS 1893 with zone V from the graph.
  - **Wind:** IS 875 Part 3 with Vb 50 m/s from the graph and terrain category 3 (assumed).
  - **Combinations:** 37 factored and 5 service combinations, including the 100% + 30% orthogonal rule.
- **Design:** IS 456 / IS 13920 concrete design, with covers from the spec (beams 30 mm, columns 40 mm).
  [CHECK: confirm on a STAAD run that the cover parameters are accepted.]

**Running STAAD.** `SProStaad.exe` is called headless (`/h`) from WSL on a `.std` text file. The run ends when
the analysis report is complete.
- **Rejected:** OpenSTAAD COM automation, which needs Windows Python and an open STAAD GUI.

**Resize loop** (`src/structure/design_loop.py`, `staad_results.py`)
- **Inputs:** the parser reads utilisation, steel required and pass/fail per member from the `.ANL`, plus
  STAAD's IS 13920 joint report.
- **Order of fixes at failing joints:**
  1. column bars first (MINMAIN)
  2. then shallower lightly reinforced beams
  3. then bigger columns
- **Practice rules:** lower columns are never smaller than the ones above; beam bars are capped at column
  size / 20 (IS 13920 cl. 7.1.1(a)); minimum column steel 0.8% is applied on the gross area (IS 456
  cl. 26.5.3.1(a)), which STAAD applies to the required area only.
- **Terrace-edge columns:** columns that stop under a terrace form their own group, so only they grow at
  single-column roof joints. On the 40×60 house this left ground columns at 350×350 instead of 450×450.
- **Typical result:** designs pass in 5–8 rounds.

**Feedback and future floors**
- **Layout feedback:** a frame that cannot pass sends a shorter maximum span back to the GA, up to three
  times (`layout_feedback`).
- **Future floors:** when the client plans another floor later, the frame is designed with the top floor
  repeated, and the footings take the future load.

**Decisions**
- *STAAD.Pro, not a custom FE solver.* IS-code member design and the strong-column check are implemented and
  recognised in practice, and an education licence was available.
  - **Rejected:** OpenSees (no IS design); ETABS (no licence); writing IS 456 design by hand (scope and risk).
- *RCDC excluded (29 October).* It is installed, but GUI-only with no API, so it can't sit in an unattended
  loop. Design stays with STAAD's own engine.
- *Deterministic loop, not LLM-guided resizing.* Structural safety must not depend on a probabilistic model.

### 5.6 Services and finishes (Phase 6)

**Plumbing** (`src/services/`)
- **Fixtures:** per wet-room template (NBC Part 9).
- **Stacks:** soil and waste stacks on the external wall face, two-pipe system.
- **Overhead tank:** on the stair-cabin roof, sized from the household (persons per dwelling from the number
  of bedrooms; storage of 1/3 to 1/2 of a day), with its weight in the STAAD frame.

**Electrical**
- **Points:** lights, fans and 6 A / 16 A points per room template, with a switch board per room and a
  distribution board per floor.
- **Limits:** sub-circuits within NBC Part 8 Sec 2 limits (10 points / 800 W per lighting sub-circuit), and
  load densities checked.
- **From the brief:** AC, TV and geyser points.
- **Accessibility:** socket heights 800–1100 mm when the household includes elderly or wheelchair users.

**Finishes.** Floor, wall, dado, skirting and ceiling per room type. The client's choices come first; gaps are
filled from the finish level (economy, standard or premium tiers, marked assumed).

**Clearances.** Stacks slide along their wall until they are 75 mm clear of columns and 40 mm of beams.

**Decision: LOD 200 from templates plus code checks.** MEP families in Revit are not loaded: fixtures and
tanks are typed DirectShapes with IFC mapping.
- **Rejected:** full MEP routing, beyond LOD 200 and the time available.

### 5.7 Building JSON (Phase 7)

- **What:** one file holds the whole design (`src/execution/layout_to_building.py`, schema v0.5), validated
  before any Revit work.
- **Decision: a single source of truth between design and Revit.**
  - **Why:** the Revit script then contains no design decisions and can be tested without Revit. The same
    file feeds the web app, the IFC consistency check and future exporters.
  - **Rejected:** generating Revit elements straight from the layout and STAAD objects.

### 5.8 Revit build (Phase 7)

- **Prototype:** a Dynamo script built walls, doors, windows, floors and rooms for a two-room flat.
- **Production: a pyRevit 7 Routes listener** inside Revit 2027 (`revit/STRATA.extension/startup.py`).
  - **Endpoints:** `/strata/status` and `/strata/build`.
  - **Build sequence:** new project from a template, then structure, walls, openings, slabs, rooms, stairs,
    pipes, fixtures, IFC export and save `.rvt`, with dialogs answered automatically.
  - **Result (19 November):** a G+1 house with 48 columns, 69 beams and 13 footings built in 47 s with no one
    at the screen.
- **Design results in Revit:** shared parameters STRATA Member, Design Status, Utilisation, Steel and Design
  Basis on columns, beams and footings, exported in the IFC property sets.
- **Consistency check** (`src/execution/ifc_check.py`): every column, beam and footing in the Revit IFC is
  found by member id. Section must match within 2 mm and position within 10 mm, and the STAAD result must be
  present.
  - **Result:** 40×60 G+1: 130/130 members, IFC checks 21/21.
- **Decisions:**
  - *pyRevit rather than a C# add-in:* no compile and deploy cycle, Python like the rest of the pipeline.
  - *pyRevit rather than Dynamo* for production: an HTTP listener allows unattended builds triggered by the
    pipeline, which Dynamo graphs don't.
  - *IronPython engine:* the one that works with Routes on Revit 2027.

### 5.9 Orchestration and web app (Phase 8)

**Orchestrator** (`src/orchestrator.py`, `scripts/strata.py`)
- **Run layout:** one folder per run and one file per stage, with a `run.json` manifest.
- **Resume:** reuses finished stages. A changed brief or a different chosen option redoes from that stage.
  During evaluation a bug was found and fixed: resuming a run that had stopped at the layout stage crashed.
- **Timing:** brief to building JSON for the 40×60 brief in about 40 s (layout 20 s, STAAD 19 s).

**Web app** (`web/`, Next.js; `src/webapi.py`)
- **Pages:** Brief (chat), Standards (cited spec), Layout (options, approval, change requests), Structure
  (live STAAD rounds), Services, Building JSON (download), Revit model (build and IFC check).
- **Hosting:** STAAD and Revit only run on the workstation. The hosted front end calls the workstation
  through a tunnel when it is up and falls back to a saved demo run otherwise (`docs/demo_runbook.md`).
- **Dropped:** a 3D IFC viewer.

**Robustness:** 14 varied briefs, no crashes (§8.4).

**Not done:** exposing STRATA as an MCP server (`design_house`, `revise_layout`) for use by any LLM (future
work).

## 7. Evaluation design

### 7.1 Questions

1. Does STRATA deliver designs that are correct (code rules, structure) where an LLM alone does not?
2. How often does it deliver a design at all, and what stops it?
3. Are its plans as good as a designer's?
4. How well does each LLM stage do its job?

### 7.2 Ablation

Three arms run on the same briefs (`scripts/eval_ablation.py`, `src/evaluation/`).

| Arm | System | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | LLM writes the model as code (Text2BIM-style, `strata_demo`: product owner, architect and coder agents) | the client's own words | IfcOpenShell script → IFC |
| B | LLM + knowledge graph: the LLM draws the whole plan from the brief and every cited requirement | gold brief + spec | layout JSON (no GA, no checker feedback) |
| C | full STRATA | gold brief | all stages to the building JSON |

**Design decisions**
- **Gold briefs.** The 25 test cases become complete briefs from their gold facts plus the matrix defaults.
  This isolates the stages after the brief; the chatbot is scored separately.
- **Same model in every arm.** Arm A runs unchanged; only its LLM call is swapped.
- **One scorer, which no arm sees while designing:**
  - the independent rule checker (pass if no code or validity rule fails)
  - reachability and programme met
  - STAAD design pass, building JSON valid
  - IFC errors for arm A
  - time and LLM calls
- **Arm A's IFC** is read back into a layout (`src/evaluation/ifc_layout.py`): rooms from IfcSpaces, room types
  from names, openings snapped to the nearest room edge. This is deliberately generous to arm A, so its
  scores are an upper bound.
- **Revit build** is checked by hand on a subset, since it is deterministic from a valid building JSON.

### 7.3 Reference comparison

There are no CPWD type designs for private plots in the reference documents. STRATA's GA was therefore
compared with hand-drawn reference plans for the same briefs (`scripts/eval_reference.py`), on rule checks,
built-up and carpet area, circulation share and mean habitable room size.

### 7.4 Model and practical limits

- **Model:** Gemma 4 26B on the free Gemini tier, temperature 0.
- **Arms A and B were too slow on it.** One arm-A brief took over two hours without finishing (repeated 503s;
  the coder reply needs more than 30 minutes).
- **Decisions:**
  - Arms A and B run on 10 briefs chosen to cover the set's variety: plot widths 20–45 ft, G+0 to G+2, shop,
    rental, wheelchair user, parking, another city. Arm C runs on all 25.
  - Arms A and B move to Groq (gpt-oss-120b) [CHECK final model]. Replies are cached per model, so models are
    never mixed in one design.
  - LLM service failures are not recorded as results.

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 What the results show

- **Reliability gap.** Every design STRATA delivered satisfies every encoded rule and passes the STAAD design;
  where it cannot, it says why and what would fit. This is the property an LLM alone does not have. Arms A and
  B quantify the difference `[after A/B]`.
- **Where reliability comes from.** It comes from the construction of the pipeline, not from the model:
  - **Grammar:** stacked grids and aligned stairs by construction.
  - **Checker:** an independent check that the generator cannot influence.
  - **Structure:** a deterministic resize loop on a recognised design engine.
  - **Revit:** a build with no decisions left in it.
- **Information gap.** The chatbot collects complete briefs in most cases (20/25). The knowledge graph fills
  the rest with cited values. Defaults are recorded as defaults, so the user can see what was assumed.
- **The price is coverage.** The GA's plan grammar trades flexibility for guaranteed structure. It works on
  40-ft plots and fails on 25–30 ft plots, which are common in Guwahati. A designer's narrow-plot form (two
  rooms side by side per band) exists and passes every rule, so the gap is in the generator, not in the codes.
- **LLM seeding gives little.** The LLM's own sketches rarely meet the NBC minimums. As seeds they don't
  improve the final layout, which suggests the LLM's value here is in understanding language, not geometry.

### 9.2 Limitations

- **The checker is STRATA's own.** It is independent of the generator, but its values come from the same
  knowledge graph; an error in the graph would pass both. The quote checker and the sign-off sheet reduce
  this risk.
- **Gold briefs in the ablation.** The end-to-end rate multiplies by the chatbot's 20/25.
- **Synthetic briefs.** 25 cases written by the author; real homeowners were not tested.
- **Assumptions to confirm:** the small-plot setback rule, FAR after the 2025 amendment, moderate exposure,
  terrain category 3, and Guwahati's climate zone (all marked assumed in the graph).
- **Structural member design** has not yet been validated by hand calculation; loads, base shear and period
  have.
- **LOD 200 services:** fixtures are DirectShapes, not MEP families; there is no pipe sizing or routing beyond
  stacks.
- **Free-tier LLM.** Its speed limited the size of the LLM arms of the evaluation.
- **Single location** (Guwahati) and a **single structural system** (RCC frame).

## 10. Conclusion and future work

STRATA shows that a homeowner's plain-language brief can be turned into a native, code-compliant,
structurally designed Revit model without a BIM engineer, provided the LLM is confined to understanding
language and every engineering decision is made by deterministic, checkable tools. Nothing STRATA delivered in
evaluation broke a rule or failed the structural design. Its coverage on narrow plots is the main open
problem.

**Future work**
- **Narrow-plot plan form** in the GA (two rooms per band, no full-length passage), and a revised bath
  default for small plots.
- **Hand-calculation validation** of member design; a STAAD run to confirm the cover parameters.
- **MCP server** (`design_house`, `revise_layout`), so any LLM assistant can use STRATA as a tool.
- **Self-hosted LLM** on Param Kamrupa (HPC) and a benchmark against the API; LoRA on the plan library as a
  comparison.
- **Vastu** as a soft objective.
- **More cities** as bylaw layers; other typologies.
- **Wider input and output:**
  - the tender/SoW/DPR path for public-works users (code exists)
  - a Revit-free IFC export (the `strata_demo` builder)
  - 4D–10D: schedule, cost from the CPWD DSR, energy, facility management, safety, lean, risk
- **Deployment** beyond a single workstation (licensing of STAAD and Revit).

## Appendix C. Glossary

- **BIM:** Building Information Modelling.
- **LOD:** level of development.
- **IFC:** Industry Foundation Classes.
- **NBC:** National Building Code of India.
- **FAR:** floor area ratio.
- **GA:** genetic algorithm.
- **KG:** knowledge graph.
- **RDF / SPARQL / SHACL:** the graph data model, its query language and its shape-validation language.
- **STAAD.Pro:** structural analysis and design software.
- **pyRevit:** Python scripting environment for Revit.
- **MCP:** Model Context Protocol.
- **SoW / DPR:** scope of work / detailed project report.
- **Mumty:** stair cabin on the roof.
- **Katha:** a land-area unit used in Assam.
